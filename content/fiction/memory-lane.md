It had been eight years since we had last seen each other.

She was late, as usual.

As I waited, I wondered why she had suddenly called me that morning.

All those high school memories were still fresh in my mind. It was my first time away from home. Although I was surrounded by people, I still felt alone.

Then I met Bhawana.

After that, I never felt lonely.

She understood me. She cared for me. She made me feel special. In college, I barely spoke to anyone besides her. It felt inevitable that I would fall in love with my best friend.

I loved the way she bit her nails whenever she was upset, her messy evening hair, her smile, her skin.

I loved everything about her.

At first, I was afraid of my feelings, but I couldn't let them go.

Our first dates, our first kiss—everything felt like a fairy tale.

Then life got in the way.

I told Bhawana our parents would never allow us to marry.

She asked me to at least ask mine.

They told me that if I married her, I would never be welcome home again.

Her parents forced her to choose between me and them.

She chose them.

I begged her to leave with me. We could move abroad. No one here would have to know about us.

But life isn't a fairy tale.

> "I choose my family."

Those words echoed inside me for years.

She asked me to forget everything. She was going to marry someone else.

I begged her to meet me one last time.

She never came.

I spent the entire day waiting for her in Basantapur.

Every promise we had made while wandering the alleys of Kumaripati remained etched in my heart, but they now felt hollow.

That night, something inside me died.

All I remember is crying.

And cursing Bhawana.

I wished I had never met her.

Then, after all those years...

She called.

---

"Kabita."

I turned around.

She was standing right behind me.

"Hello, Kabita. How are you?"

"I'm good."

"I'm sorry I'm late. Traffic was terrible."

We sat down.

She ordered a plate of momos.

I only asked for coffee.

Silence settled between us.

Bhawana tried talking about the weather, a new novel by Buddhisagar, the latest songs—anything to avoid the real reason we were there.

Finally, she spoke.

> "I'm sorry, Kabita. For every pain I caused you. Please don't hate me.

> I miss you. Every single day.

> I still love you.

> I know it sounds wrong. I'm married now. I thought marriage would help me move on, but it never did.

> It took me years to gather the courage to see you again.

> Can we be together?"

I looked at her for a long moment.

"Bhawana...

Everything we shared will always stay with me.

You were family.

I cannot hate you.

I know you made your choice because you had no other choice.

I respect that.

But we cannot be together now.

Talking about the past won't help either of us.

It won't help our families."

She looked down.

"I've spent my whole life trying to make everyone else happy," she whispered.

"Now I want my share of happiness."

"I understand," I said.

"But I cannot be the reason your family falls apart.

After you left, I was shattered.

I fell into depression.

Then I met him.

My psychologist.

He stood beside me during the darkest days of my life.

Eventually, he became my partner.

He knows everything about me.

About us.

He knows my sexuality.

He taught me to embrace who I am.

He loved me without conditions.

Now we have a three-year-old son named Arun.

Viren knows I met you today.

When you called, I didn't know whether I should come.

If it weren't for him, I probably would have hated you forever.

He helped me understand that you were forced to choose between me and your family.

You always put others before yourself.

That says everything about the person you are.

If there's ever another way I can help you...

I will."

Bhawana remained silent.

She stared at the grass beneath her feet.

Finally, I smiled.

"Thank you...

For every beautiful memory."

She smiled back.

"I'm happy you're happy, Kabita.

Stay happy."

"Thank you.

Viren has invited you home sometime.

Arun would love to meet you."

"I'd like that," she said softly.

"Someday."

---

Fifteen years have passed since that afternoon.

Sometimes I still sit alone in my garden, wandering down that familiar memory lane.

Bhawana never called again.

That brief meeting in the little café is all I have left of us.

"Mother!" Arun shouts from the balcony.

"Baba says the barbecue is ready!"

I smile.

"Coming, son."

I brush the dust from my clothes and walk back home.
