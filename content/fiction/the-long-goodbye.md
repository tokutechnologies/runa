# Dear Maanu,

I am glad your uncle Ramesh took this photograph of us.

The first time I got to hold my princess.

I felt that your sparkling eyes were searching for me—that you recognised me. I felt that you would say *Baba* at that very moment. You were not crying—just looking at me.

I was already a proud father with you in my arms.

I promised myself then that I would always be there for you.

This one is a picture of your first day at school.

You were so excited to wear your new school uniform. I had ironed it twice at your request—a blue shirt and a white skirt. Your mother dressed you up so neatly. Then you came to me to show off your new look and your new school bag.

You looked ready to take on the world.

But that was short-lived.

No sooner had the school bus arrived than you broke down in tears. The way you cried over my shoulders, I thought I would never send you to school.

Thankfully, your mother had the courage to carry you to the bus. Otherwise, I would never have let you go.

Here is another photograph.

This one is from when you were recovering from typhoid.

I remember that night as if it were yesterday.

Your mother and I rushed you to the hospital in the middle of the night after you threw up everything you ate.

That entire week you were in the hospital, I could not sleep.

It felt as though a storm was raging inside my mind, and it did not settle until you had fully recovered.

There is little else you can do when things are no longer in your control.

I still have nightmares about the day your mother left us.

That ordinary Sunday morning suddenly turned our lives upside down.

At that street corner, when her car was hit by a truck, I was told she died instantly.

I was not there with her.

Your mother and I used to say that if we ever had to leave this world, we would leave it together.

At that time, I could not have survived if you had not stood beside me.

I know you cried all night.

I could hear you screaming your mother's name.

But the next morning, you wore a brave face.

When I could not even hold myself together, you believed in me.

Every single day you reminded me that I had to move on.

That I had to be brave.

You told me your mother would never want to see me broken.

You kept saying everything would be alright.

At least we still had each other.

I would have forgotten how to live if it were not for you holding my hand.

My daughter, you were brave.

You took care of me as though you were my mother.

That was when I realised my daughter was no ordinary person.

She was a lioness, just like her mother.

I knew you could face anything.

Look here.

This is a photograph from your fifth birthday.

Your mother and I had given you a white coat because you wanted to become a doctor.

At seven, you wanted to become a pilot.

You loved watching airplanes fly overhead.

You were determined to fly one someday.

By thirteen, you wanted to become an engineer.

Sometimes I wonder who you would have become if November 27, 2012 had never happened.

I am so sorry, Maanu.

I could not be the father you deserved.

On November 27, 2012, while we were supposed to be cutting your birthday cake, I was instead called to identify your body.

I remember only shock.

My heart felt as though it would explode.

My head burned.

My body felt trapped inside a cage.

I cried once.

Then there was only darkness.

You had told me before that there were boys at your college who were making you uncomfortable.

Later, you told me you could handle it yourself.

I wish I had not waited.

If only I had gone to your college that very day, I would still have my daughter.

Everything would be different.

I was so foolish.

I am so sorry, Maanu.

Your father was not there when you needed him the most.

I could not keep my promise.

After that day, I do not remember living.

I only remember breathing.

Now all those animals who tortured my flower are behind bars.

I made sure that every person who made you suffer suffered as well.

But there is no solace in retribution.

Three years have not eased my pain.

But this morning, I realised I could finally be happy again.

After three years of living like a nobody, I have decided not to live anymore.

I had thought you and your mother, Rekha, were important parts of my life.

Now I know you both *were* my life.

I wonder why my loves were taken away from me so early.

Why did both of you have to die that way?

Why are people so cruel?

Don't they realise their actions destroy other people's lives?

Why don't they treasure the lives of others?

I don't want to stay here anymore.

I choose to be with my family.

I have already fought my battle.

Ironically, as I stand at the very edge, I have the lines from your favourite song stuck in my head.

> There is a better place than this. Emptiness.

Lots of love,

**Baba**
